Prices in the open

What does a website cost?

The honest answer is “it depends”. Except that isn’t an answer — it’s a way of moving the conversation to a phone call. Below are the figures I actually build quotes from, line by line, with no form to fill in before you can see them.

The short answer

For a local business that needs to be found on Google and get phone calls, a website costs between €450 and €575. Once, not yearly. More languages, a menu, bookings or a shop push the figure up — and below you can see by exactly how much.

These figures update themselves: they are the same ones that appear on a quote, not a separate shop window.

Four cases, four figures

These aren’t packages to choose from. They are the four cases that come up most often, with what they cost.

A single page

€275 – 350

Opening hours, contact, map and a form. The calling card that’s missing when all you have is a Facebook page.

Up to five pages

€450 – 575

The most common case: who you are, what you do, your work, how to reach you. Built for you, not a template filled in.

Five pages in three languages

€675 – 875

Italian, German and English. For anyone working with visitors or abroad — real text in each language, not machine translation.

Online shop

€1,000 – 1,350

Catalogue, basket, card payment. What it ends up costing depends mostly on how many products there are.

On top, if you want it: care from €39 a month. It’s optional, it’s a separate contract, and the site runs just the same without it.

What comes back every year

The part almost nobody puts on their own website — and that turns up later as a surprise.

Domain
€15 – 30 a year, registered in your name. Change supplier one day and you take it with you: it isn’t mine.
Hosting and certificate
Included in the monthly care. On its own, with a decent provider, it’s roughly €50 – 90 a year.
Care
€39 a month, optional: security updates, backups, uptime checks, and the small changes to text, hours and photos.
What there isn’t
No hidden standing charge, no annual licence on a system I built myself, no fee for getting your own files back.

Doing it yourself, one person, an agency

Three routes, three sets of numbers. None of them is wrong: it depends on how much time you have and how much the site has to carry.

Yourself, with a website builder

€150 – 400 a year

Cheap and immediate. The real bill is your own time, and the site stays a guest on a platform: stop paying and it’s gone.

One person

€500 – 2,500 once

You talk to whoever builds it. Lower prices because there’s no apparatus to fund. The risk is availability — always ask who answers the phone in two years.

An agency

€2,000 – 8,000 once

Several people, more hands and more process. It makes sense for large projects; for a local business you’re mostly paying for the structure.

The questions that always come

Why isn’t there a price list with three packages?

Because packages serve the seller, not the buyer. A package always contains something you don’t need and leaves out the one thing you do — and the conversation turns into choosing the lesser evil. Here you pay for the items that concern you and none of the others.

Can the figure on the quote change along the way?

No. The quote lists every item with its price; once you accept it, that total is the final price. If you want to add something midway, I price it first and you decide. Nothing appears on the invoice that wasn’t on the quote.

How long does it take?

A five-page site usually one to two weeks, trilingual or with extra functions two to three. The pace isn’t set by my work but by the material: if the text and photographs exist, it moves fast.

Do I have to pay it all up front?

Half on accepting the quote, half on handover. Care, if you take it, starts the month after the site goes live.

Is the site mine even without the care contract?

Yes. Code, images and project files are handed over, the domain is in your name, and there’s no technical lock-in. Without care the site runs exactly the same — updates and backups are simply down to you.

Do you only work in the province of Agrigento?

I work from Aragona, and clients in the province get a handshake. But the work is just as good at a distance: I have clients in Italy, Germany, Austria and Switzerland, and the site is handed over in Italian, German and English.
